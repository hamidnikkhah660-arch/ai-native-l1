Ten public release safeguards

These ten safeguards communicate the standard of evidence required before a public-network release. They are intentionally expressed as outcome-level acceptance criteria; they do not publish internal implementation recipes, operational endpoints, key material or detailed threat models.

  1. Release authority: the network launch scope must be explicitly approved; the current signed authorization is limited to zero-value Public Testnet.
  2. Consistent release package: all canonical network, protocol and monetary artifacts must be complete, mutually consistent and independently checked.
  3. Trusted network entry: initial peers and their identities must be independently verified; placeholder or unverified bootstrap information is unacceptable.
  4. Independent operation: multiple operators must control genuinely separate infrastructure and failure domains.
  5. External connectivity: inbound and outbound network behaviour must be demonstrated from independent external networks.
  6. Identity and key safety: peer identity, protected key custody, restart behaviour and downgrade resistance must pass security checks.
  7. History consistency: independent nodes must converge on valid network history and reject inconsistent or untrusted state.
  8. Durability and recovery: persistence, restart, catch-up and recovery must be reproducible under realistic failure conditions.
  9. Usable interfaces: wallet, developer access, explorer, monitoring and any advertised execution environment must be available and tested to the scope claimed.
  10. Independent release review: protocol, cryptography, economics, build reproducibility and operating procedures must receive documented external review and a formal go/no-go decision.

These safeguards describe release outcomes, not the project's complete design. Passing one does not imply the others have passed. Public Testnet and Mainnet each require their own scope, evidence and authorization.

Active monetary policy — CP-0651

The active candidate policy is a 7,000,000,000 QZXB maximum accounting ceiling, comprising a 1,000,000,000 QZXB owner Genesis allocation and six additional 1B non-circulating accounting labels. The labels are not issued or transferable balances and do not authorize minting. Block subsidy is zero; the candidate producer compensation model is canonical transaction fees only. CP-0651 supersedes the older CP-0437 10B-to-one-owner design and the historical CP-0604 mining allocation model.

CP-0651-A v1.0 was merged into main on 27 November 2026. It sets the founder release candidate at 10% (100M QZXB) transferable only at authorized Mainnet launch and the remaining 90% (900M) linearly over 24 months from launch, with no additional cliff. The mechanism must still be implemented, tested and authorized before activation. See the complete allocation and release policy.

The value-bearing Mainnet package and its separate authorization are not complete.

PR #198 is open and unmerged. Its CI result applies only to its 19 November head, not to the later CP-0795–CP-0807 work. No public deployment or restart is claimed by this update.

Private overlay versus public Internet ingress

A prior two-host private-overlay qualification is retained as evidence for that limited environment. Separate external-ingress checks did not establish unsolicited public inbound reachability. These results answer different questions: a successful private-overlay test does not prove that an independently hosted public network is reachable.

Independent public hosts, verified endpoints, protected node identities and operator evidence are not yet provisioned for release. Private NetBird tests and same-host loopback recovery tests remain useful scoped evidence, but they do not prove independent public-Internet operation or separate failure domains. No endpoint, key or independent review is claimed without evidence.

Market and platform status

No authorized native QZXB Mainnet, verified public circulating balance, live QZXB/USDT market reserve, or exchange trade is claimed. The QX ERC-20 deployment on Ethereum Sepolia is a separate test asset and must not be described as native QZXB.

CoinMarketCap's current tracked-listing guidance calls for a functional website and block explorer plus active public trading with material volume on at least one CMC-tracked exchange; untracked/preview discovery is a distinct category and is not the same as a tracked ranked asset. TradingView does not let users manually add unsupported symbols to its main platform. A custom Advanced Charts integration can use the project's own datafeed, while actual Trading Platform order entry additionally requires a broker backend.
